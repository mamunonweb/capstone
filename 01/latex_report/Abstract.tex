\begin{center}
    \section*{Abstract}
\end{center}
Finding potato leaf diseases early and correctly is important for food security and helps farmers lose less money. Deep Learning (DL) models can detect diseases from leaf photos. But many of these models are large, run slowly on cheap devices and are hard to trust, because they work like a ``black box''. In this study we design a small custom Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) with Swish activation, Batch Normalization and Global Average Pooling (162,887 parameters, about 2 MB). We compare it with ResNet50 and MobileNetV2 on the Potato Leaf Disease Dataset in Uncontrolled Environment. The dataset has 3,076 field images in 7 classes (Bacteria, Fungi, Healthy, Nematode, Pest, Phytopthora and Virus). We split it into training, validation and test sets (80:10:10) with stratified sampling. All models were trained in a Kaggle notebook on the CPU for only a few epochs, and the two benchmark models started without ImageNet weights. Under these conditions, ResNet50 got the highest test accuracy, 31.82\% (micro-average AUC 0.7688). MobileNetV2 got 24.35\% (AUC 0.6652) and the Custom CNN got 19.81\% (AUC 0.5889). The Custom CNN was the smallest model (1.98 MB). ResNet50 was the largest (92.1 MB) and the slowest (136.7 ms per image). The confusion matrices show that the Custom CNN and MobileNetV2 predicted only one class, so these models did not converge. We added Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) for explanation, and a Large Language Model (Google Gemini) that turns the predicted class and confidence into simple advice for farmers. The results show that field photos with busy backgrounds are much harder than lab photos. The next steps must be pre-trained weights, GPU training, longer training and class balancing. The study gives a complete pipeline that others can repeat, and it can be retrained to reach useful accuracy.
