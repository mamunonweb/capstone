\begin{center}
    \section*{Abstract}
\end{center}
Early and accurate diagnosis of potato leaf diseases is important for food security and for reducing economic loss in agriculture. Deep Learning (DL) models can detect diseases from leaf images, but many models are large, slow on low-cost devices and hard to trust, because they work as a ``black box''. This study designs a lightweight custom Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) with Swish activation, Batch Normalization and Global Average Pooling (162,887 parameters, about 2 MB) and compares it with ResNet50 and MobileNetV2 on the Potato Leaf Disease Dataset in Uncontrolled Environment. The dataset contains 3,076 field images in 7 classes (Bacteria, Fungi, Healthy, Nematode, Pest, Phytopthora and Virus) and was divided into training, validation and test sets (80:10:10) with stratified sampling. All models were trained in a Kaggle notebook on the CPU for a small number of epochs, and the two benchmark models were initialized without ImageNet weights. Under these conditions, ResNet50 reached the highest test accuracy of 31.82\% (micro-average AUC 0.7688), followed by MobileNetV2 with 24.35\% (AUC 0.6652) and the Custom CNN with 19.81\% (AUC 0.5889). The Custom CNN was the smallest model (1.98 MB), while ResNet50 was the largest (92.1 MB) and the slowest (136.7 ms per image). The confusion matrices show that the Custom CNN and MobileNetV2 predicted only one class, which means these models did not converge. Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) was implemented for explainability, and a Large Language Model (Google Gemini) was integrated to turn the predicted class and confidence into farmer-friendly advice. The results show that field images with complex backgrounds are much harder than laboratory images, and that pre-trained weights, GPU training, longer training and class balancing are the necessary next steps. The study delivers a complete and reproducible pipeline that can be retrained to reach practical accuracy.
