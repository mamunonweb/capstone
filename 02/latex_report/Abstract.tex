\begin{center}
    \section*{\textbf{Abstract}}
\end{center}
Finding potato leaf diseases early and correctly helps small farmers protect their harvest and use fewer chemicals. Modern Deep Learning (DL) models are accurate on farm images, but many of the best ones are large ensembles. They are expensive to run on the web or on a phone, and they work like a ``black box'': they do not show the farmer why they made a decision or what to do next. In this thesis we build a potato leaf disease detector that is accurate, small and explainable, together with an advice system. It uses a small fine-tuned MobileNetV2 model (2,264,389 parameters, 8.64~MB) and runs as a web application for farmers in English and Bengali.

We use a 5-class field dataset of 2,321 potato leaf photos (Bacteria, Fungi, Healthy, Phytopthora and Virus). Near-duplicate photos are grouped with perceptual difference hashing ($\text{dHash} \le 10$), which gives 2,283 groups. The groups are then split 80:10:10 with \texttt{StratifiedGroupKFold}, so no leaf appears in two splits. This gives 1,859 training, 231 validation and 231 unseen test images. We train the model on two NVIDIA Tesla T4 GPUs. We use capped class weights, leaf-like geometric and color augmentation and two-stage AdamW fine-tuning. Before Stage~2, we reload the best head checkpoint from Stage~1 and unfreeze the top 54 backbone layers. All Batch Normalization layers stay frozen, which stops the sudden loss jump (fine-tuning shock) that we saw in our first run.

On the leak-aware test set, our 8.64~MB MobileNetV2 reaches \textbf{93.51\%} single-pass accuracy, \textbf{0.9272} macro-F1 and \textbf{0.4466} test loss. With 4-view Test-Time Augmentation, which adds no parameters, it reaches \textbf{93.94\%} accuracy and \textbf{0.9317} macro-F1. The exported model gives exactly the same output as the trained one ($0.00 \times 10^{0}$ maximum probability difference). We compare it with three larger or different models on the same test split, and it is both the smallest and the most accurate. The MultiNet-V3 ensemble (EfficientNetB0 + MobileNetV2 + ResNet50, 31.08M parameters, 118.55~MB) is 13.7$\times$ larger and scores 90.91\% accuracy and 0.8975 macro-F1. InceptionV3 (85.18~MB) scores 84.42\% and 0.8296 macro-F1, and NASNetMobile (17.33~MB) scores 72.73\% and 0.7049 macro-F1. Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM) on the last convolutional layer (\texttt{Conv\_1}) shows that the model looks at the lesions. A Google Gemini module with safety rules turns each prediction into practical advice. Everything runs in a live FastAPI and React web application with camera capture, batch analysis, PDF/CSV export and chat in two languages.
